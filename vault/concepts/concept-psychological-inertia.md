## Overview

**Psychological inertia (PI)** is the cognitive tendency to stay within familiar,
domain-specific solution paths — to think in the direction one is used to — which
blocks access to solutions in other fields or at higher invention levels. In TRIZ
it is the principal *human* obstacle to invention, distinct from the technical
difficulty of the problem.

## Key Properties

- PI is why most engineers default to Level 1–2 solutions (see
  [[concept-levels-of-invention|Levels of Invention]]): they search only within
  their own specialty, the region that yields the least inventive results.
- TRIZ counters PI structurally: the [[concept-ideality|Ideal Final Result]]
  forces reasoning backward from the perfect outcome; the **Size–Time–Cost (STC)
  operator** stretches parameters to 0 and ∞; **Smart Little People** modeling and
  the [[concept-40-inventive-principles|40 Principles]] supply cross-domain prompts.
- [[concept-ariz|ARIZ]] embeds PI-suppression steps (e.g. restating the problem in
  jargon-free everyday language).
- The phrase "vector of psychological inertia" is used in practitioner literature
  but is **unverified** as a verbatim Altshuller coinage; the underlying concept
  (PI as a directional bias) is well attested.

## Connections
