## Overview

The **TRIZ Effects Database** maps *desired functions* (e.g. "move a liquid,"
"measure temperature") to the *physical, chemical, and geometric effects* that
can deliver them. Unlike an encyclopedia indexed by discipline, it inverts the
lookup so an engineer starts from "what function do I need?" and retrieves
cross-domain phenomena that achieve it — directly attacking
[[concept-psychological-inertia|psychological inertia]].

## Key Properties

- **Organized by function, not by industry or branch of science** — its defining
  feature.
- Developmental history: a physical-effects handbook (1979); chemical effects
  (Salamatov, 1988); inventive use of geometry (Vikentiev & Efremov, 1989); later
  digitized/translated (Invention Machine's TechOptimizer/Goldfire; Oxford
  Creativity's database). Classic classes: **physical, chemical, geometric**;
  later extended with **biological** effects.
- Supplies the "field" half of [[concept-su-field-analysis|su-field]] models and
  the "introduce a field/effect" standards; invoked in
  [[concept-ariz|ARIZ]]'s solution-search steps.
- Level-5 inventions (see [[concept-levels-of-invention|Levels of Invention]]) are
  precisely the discovery of *new* effects that later populate this database.

## Connections

Operationalizes [[concept-ideality|ideality]] by finding the simplest physical
principle for a function.
