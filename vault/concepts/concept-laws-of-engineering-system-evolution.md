## Overview

The **Laws of Engineering (Technical) System Evolution** are general, objective
patterns Altshuller claimed govern how technical systems develop over time —
derived from large-scale patent analysis. They are both **predictive** (forecast
where a system is heading) and **prescriptive** (point toward the next
evolutionary state when solving a problem). Consolidated in the mid-to-late 1970s
(first-publication year reported inconsistently as 1975 vs 1979 — flagged).

## Key Properties — Altshuller's 8 laws, in 3 blocks

**Statics (viability of a new system):**
1. Completeness of the parts — a viable system needs engine, transmission,
   working unit, and control element; at least one controllable.
2. Energy conductivity — energy must flow through all main parts.
3. Harmonization (coordination) of rhythms — frequencies/periodicities coordinated.

**Kinematics (evolution independent of conditions):**
4. Increasing [[concept-ideality|ideality]].
5. Uneven development of parts — generates the
   [[concept-technical-contradiction|contradictions]] that drive evolution.
6. Transition to a [[concept-super-system|super-system]].

**Dynamics (evolution under specific conditions):**
7. Transition from macro to micro level.
8. Increasing involvement of substance-fields
   ([[concept-su-field-analysis|su-field]]) / controllability — exact canonical
   title varies (flagged).

## Laws vs. modern "Trends" (TESE)

MATRIZ uses **"Trends of Engineering Systems Evolution" (TESE)** — a larger,
reformulated set (commonly ~11 trends: S-curve, increasing value, super-system
transition, completeness, decreasing human involvement, trimming, flow
enhancement, coordination, uneven development, controllability, dynamization),
deliberately calling them *trends* (tendencies) rather than *laws*. Some scholars
critique the original "laws" as "productive tautologies" rather than empirical
laws.

## Connections

The macro life-cycle is the [[concept-s-curve|S-curve]]; the laws describe the
mechanisms along it.
