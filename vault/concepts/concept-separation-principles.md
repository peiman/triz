## Overview

The **Separation Principles** are the primary family of methods for resolving a
[[concept-physical-contradiction|physical contradiction]] — where a single
parameter must take two opposite values. Rather than compromise, they **separate
the conflicting requirements** so the opposite values never coexist.

## Key Properties

- **Western canon (4 separations)** — Altshuller → Domb → Mann:
  - **In time** — +P at one moment, −P at another (e.g. retractable landing gear).
  - **In space** — +P in one region, −P in another (e.g. a knife: sharp edge,
    thick spine).
  - **Upon condition** — +P under one condition, −P under another (e.g. a sieve:
    blocks large objects, passes small ones).
  - **Between system levels (parts ↔ whole)** — whole has one value, parts the
    opposite (e.g. a bicycle chain: flexible as a whole, rigid per link).
- **Count is source-dependent (flagged):** modern MATRIZ lists 5+ methods (adds
  *Separation in Direction*; treats *Relation* ≈ *Condition*; *System level*
  applies always). The boundary between "upon condition" / "in relation" / "by
  scale" is fuzzy across sources.
- **Mapping to the [[concept-40-inventive-principles|40 Principles]]:** each
  separation recommends a subset of principles. The MATRIZ algorithm's mapping is
  verified; the classic Domb/Mann per-separation lists differ and exact subsets
  outside MATRIZ are **unverified** — reconcile against the primary book before
  citing exact numbers.
- Applied within [[concept-ariz|ARIZ]] at the point where it has exposed the
  physical contradiction.
