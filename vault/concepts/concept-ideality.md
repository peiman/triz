## Overview

**Ideality** is TRIZ's central measure of system quality: the ratio of all useful
(beneficial) effects a system delivers to the sum of its harmful effects and
costs. The **Ideal Final Result (IFR)** is the implementation-free description of
the situation *after* the problem is solved — the limiting ideal where the system
delivers all its benefits with no cost, no harm, and ideally no system at all
("the function is performed but the machine is absent"). TRIZ holds that technical
systems evolve toward increasing ideality.

## Key Properties

- **Ideality equation:** `Ideality = Σ(useful functions / benefits) ÷ [ Σ(harmful
  functions / harm) + Σ(costs) ]` — i.e. benefits ÷ (costs + harm). Raise benefits
  and/or lower costs and harm to increase ideality. (Function/benefit terminology
  varies across sources; same idea.)
- The **IFR is stated at the start** of problem solving to set the target, then
  the solver works backward — countering psychological inertia and avoiding
  anchoring on existing, compromised solutions.
- A well-formed IFR eliminates the deficiency, preserves existing advantages, adds
  no new complexity, and ideally uses **free/already-available resources**.
- The asymptotic "ideal machine" has no weight, volume, energy use, or
  maintenance — a *direction of evolution*, not a literally reachable end-state.
- Ideality is one of the **Laws/Trends of Engineering System Evolution**.

## Critique (flagged)

Some scholars argue "the ideal IFR is no IFR" — defining the ideal as the
system's own disappearance can be self-undermining as an operational target
(critique literature noted but not fully verified here).
