## Overview

The canonical [[concept-su-field-analysis|Su-Field]] **teaching example**,
demonstrating the [[concept-76-standard-solutions|76 Standard Solutions]]
end-to-end (credited in-source to Lev Shulyak).

## Problem

A pneumatic hammer (piston driven by pulsed compressed air) breaks rock. The
function works, but it produces a **harmful effect: flying rock fragments** (and
over-fragmentation).

## TRIZ framing

- Modeled as a **su-field**: **S1 = rock** (object), **S2 = hammer** (tool),
  **F = mechanical field** (pulsed air). The interaction yields a useful effect
  (rock broken) plus a harmful effect (flying particles).
- Model state: a **harmful complete system** → eliminate the negative effect.

## Solution & tools

- Per the Standard Solutions (harmful-complete-system, **subgroup 1.2**): either
  **introduce a substance S3** (a wire mesh / metal cap to contain fragments), or
  **introduce/change a field** — **freeze the rock** so internal moisture expands
  and cracks it, breaking it gently (a zero-cost variant: let winter freezing do
  it). The freeze route also lowers the mechanical effort (a "super-effect").
- Quantified outcome: **not documented** (method illustration).
