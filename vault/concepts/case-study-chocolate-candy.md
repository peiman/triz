## Overview

A classic TRIZ **teaching example** (not a documented industrial deployment) of
resolving a [[concept-physical-contradiction|physical contradiction]] via the
[[concept-ideality|Ideal Final Result]].

## Problem

A line fills chocolate shells with a hot liqueur-and-sugar syrup. Heating the
filling makes it flow and fill faster — but the hot filling **melts the chocolate
shell**.

## TRIZ framing

- **Physical contradiction:** the filling must be *hot* (to flow and fill quickly)
  and *not hot* (so it does not melt the shell).
- **Ideal Final Result (as documented):** "the goo is on the inside and the
  chocolate is on the outside" — stated independent of mechanism.
- *The specific 39-parameter matrix pair is **not** documented in the source —
  the case leads with the IFR, not a matrix lookup.*

## Solution & tools

- **[[principle-35-parameter-changes|Principle 35: Parameter Changes]]** (change
  physical state): **freeze the filling into its final shape first, then dip the
  frozen core into melted chocolate** — no hot liquid ever touches the shell.
  [[principle-36-phase-transitions|Principle 36]] is commonly co-cited.
- Quantified outcome: **not documented** (method illustration).

## Source

IFR framing, physical contradiction, and freeze-then-dip solution; the matrix
parameter pair and any production metrics are unverified.
