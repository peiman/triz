## Overview

A meta-note on how TRIZ is used in industry — and an honest caveat about the
evidence. A critical survey of 200+ TRIZ case papers
([[source-spreafico-russo-2016-survey|Spreafico & Russo 2016]]) found the
literature splits sharply:

- **Impact-only corporate anecdotes** — large ROI / patent figures, but no
  reproducible contradiction→principle method.
- **Method-complete academic papers** — the full method (e.g.
  [[case-study-pcr-diagnostics|the PCR case]]), but small problems and rarely any
  ROI.

## The impact-only cases (method proprietary)

- **Samsung Electronics:** enterprise TRIZ program (~2000s); per Forbes, early
  projects "saved over $100 million," TRIZ "led to 50 new patents" in 2003, and a
  2004 DVD-pickup project saved >$100M; 1,000+ engineers trained. Impact only —
  **no published contradiction/principle breakdown**
  ([[source-samsung-forbes-2013|Forbes 2013]], journalistic, medium confidence).
- **Intel:** a widely-repeated claim of ">$212.5M ROI, verified by Intel Finance,
  over a 21-month deployment." **Provenance is weak** — it traces to a single
  self-published (non-peer-reviewed) post; treat as unverified. No method
  documented.

## Takeaway

The strongest *method* evidence is academic (small-scale, reproducible); the
strongest *impact* evidence is corporate (large, but unverifiable in method).
Adopter-name lists (P&G, Boeing, GE, Ford, etc.) commonly circulate but lack
primary method-level sources — not asserted here.
