## Overview

Altshuller's classification of inventive solutions by **degree of
inventiveness**, derived from patent analysis (a corpus of ~40,000 selected from
~200,000 abstracts). The distinguishing criterion is *how far outside the
solver's own field the required knowledge lies*, which correlates with the rough
number of trial-and-error attempts.

## Key Properties

| Level | Label | Knowledge source / trials | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Apparent/routine | within the specialty; ~1–10 trials; no real contradiction | ~32% |
| 2 | Minor improvement | within the industry; resolves a contradiction by known means; ~10–100 | ~45% |
| 3 | Substantial improvement | from outside the industry; ~100–1,000 | ~18% |
| 4 | New generation/concept | from science, not technology; ~1k–100k | ~4% |
| 5 | Discovery/pioneering | a newly discovered phenomenon; founds a discipline; ~100k–1M+ | ~1% |

- Percentages **32 / 45 / 18 / 4 / 1** are the most-cited figures (Levels 1/3/4
  directly confirmed; 2 and 5 corroborated and arithmetically complete but not
  primary-verified — flagged).
- Only Levels 1–2 are reliably solvable from a solver's existing knowledge;
  Levels 3–5 require crossing field boundaries — exactly what TRIZ tools
  systematize. Level-5 discoveries become new entries in the
  [[concept-effects-database|effects database]].
- English level labels vary by translation (criteria are stable).

## Connections
