## Overview

TRIZ holds that a technical system's key performance parameter, plotted against
time (or cumulative effort), traces an **S-shaped (logistic) curve** with four
life-cycle stages: **infancy** (slow, inefficient start), **growth** (steep rise
once a key barrier falls), **maturity** (gains flatten at physical limits), and
**decline** (displaced by a next-generation system, starting a new S-curve).

## Key Properties

- The S-curve is the macro-level descriptive envelope; the
  [[concept-laws-of-engineering-system-evolution|laws of evolution]] describe the
  mechanisms driving change along it.
- Approaching maturity, evolution is driven by increasing
  [[concept-ideality|ideality]], uneven development (contradictions), transition
  to a [[concept-super-system|super-system]], and the macro→micro transition —
  which seed the next S-curve.
- Altshuller proposed locating a system on its S-curve via four sub-curves over
  time: number of inventions/patents, level of inventiveness, performance, and
  profitability.
