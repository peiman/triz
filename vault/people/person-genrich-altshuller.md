## About

Genrikh Saulovich Altshuller (1926, Tashkent – 1998, Petrozavodsk) was the Soviet
engineer, inventor, and writer who founded **TRIZ** — *Teoriya Resheniya
Izobretatelskikh Zadach*, the Theory of Inventive Problem Solving. He developed
the method's founding insight while working as a patent consultant for the
Caspian Sea Military Navy: studying large numbers of patents to find generic,
teachable rules underlying inventive solutions, he concluded that every hard
technical problem embodies a **contradiction** (see
[[concept-technical-contradiction|Technical Contradiction]]).

He also wrote science fiction under the pen name **Genrich Altov** — the same
person, not a separate author; his popular TRIZ problem book *And Suddenly the
Inventor Appeared* ([[source-altshuller-1994-inventor-appeared|Altshuller 1994]])
was published under that name.

## Key Contributions

- Founded TRIZ; published the first paper in 1956 with R. B. Shapiro
  ([[source-altshuller-shapiro-1956|Altshuller & Shapiro 1956]]).
- Developed the [[concept-contradiction-matrix|Contradiction Matrix]],
  [[concept-39-engineering-parameters|39 Engineering Parameters]],
  [[concept-40-inventive-principles|40 Inventive Principles]], and the principle
  of [[concept-ideality|Ideality / Ideal Final Result]].
- Authored the major TRIZ works:
  [[source-altshuller-1984-creativity-exact-science|Creativity as an Exact Science]],
  [[source-altshuller-1999-innovation-algorithm|The Innovation Algorithm]], and
  [[source-altshuller-1994-inventor-appeared|And Suddenly the Inventor Appeared]].
- Founded the Azerbaijan Public Institute of Inventive Creativity (1971).

## Contested biographical details (flagged, unverified against primary sources)

- **Letter to Stalin:** widely reported as December 1948 (with R. Shapiro),
  criticizing the USSR's unsystematic approach to invention; not affirmed by
  English Wikipedia — treat as reported, not confirmed.
- **Arrest:** c. 1950; sentenced under a "inventor's sabotage" charge;
  imprisoned in the Gulag (incl. Vorkuta).
- **Release year:** **contested** — commonly cited as 1954, but the Altshuller
  Institute biography implies ~1956 (about 18 months after Stalin's 1953 death).
  Do not assert a single year without a primary source.
